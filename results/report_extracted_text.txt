Categorical Encoding and Threshold Selection for BRFSS Classiﬁcation
Mohamed Amine Hmidi Mohamed Y ebari Omar Marouane Bensaid
EPFL
Abstract—We classify self-reported myocardial infarction
or coronary heart disease in BRFSS-derived survey data
using NumPy implementations of linear models. We compare
imputation, missingness indicators, categorical encoding and
regularization on a stratiﬁed development/tuning split, reserv-
ing an independent ﬁnal holdout. Reference encoding of small
integer-valued feature sets improves tuning F1 by 0.0147 at
a common threshold. Our selected L2 logistic model attains
holdout F1 0.4225, accuracy 0.8685, and recall 0.5446 at a
tuning-selected threshold of 0.20. Threshold choice dominates
the measured accuracy–recall tradeoff. These observational,
self-reported outcomes support classiﬁcation comparisons, not
prospective clinical claims.
I. D ATA AND EVALUATION DESIGN
The supplied dataset contains 328,135 labeled observa-
tions and 109,379 test observations, each with 321 features.
There are 28,975 positive training labels (8.8302%). The
target _MICHD describes respondents reporting a history
of myocardial infarction or coronary heart disease, derived
from the corresponding survey questions [ 1]. It does not es-
tablish prospective incidence or clinically veriﬁed diagnosis.
Always predicting the negative class obtains 0.9117 accuracy
but zero recall and F1.
Raw feature missingness is 44.7880% in training and
44.7852% in test. No inﬁnite values, duplicate IDs, train/test
ID overlap, or exact duplicate rows across all 321 raw
features were found. Feature names and order match be-
tween splits, and label IDs align with training inputs. These
checks do not establish that distinct rows represent indepen-
dent respondents. Many survey codes have variable-speciﬁc
meanings; for example, 88 denotes zero unhealthy days
for PHYSHLTH [1]. We therefore do not apply a universal
sentinel rule.
Using seed 433, we stratify labeled rows into 196,881
development, 65,627 tuning, and 65,627 untouched hold-
out observations. Every ﬁll value, scale, category set, and
feature-removal choice is ﬁtted only on development rows
during selection. Ten conﬁgurations and the selection rule
were recorded before ﬁtting. The AIcrowd rules specify
accuracy, while the observed leaderboard lists F1 ﬁrst and
appears ordered by F1 [ 2], [ 3]. Pending clariﬁcation, we
provisionally optimize F1 locally and report accuracy sepa-
rately.
II. M ETHODS
Feature processing. We remove the input ID and exclude
annual sequence number SEQNO, its duplicate _PSU, and
raw interview date IDATE. We also exclude HAREHAB1:
its rehabilitation question is conditional on a reported heart
attack [1]. All 773 observed training values occur in positive
cases, making availability a target proxy. Other medical-
history predictors are retained.
Numeric columns receive a development-ﬁtted mean or
median ﬁll followed by mean/std scaling; fully observed
constants and all-missing columns are removed. Optional
binary indicators represent missingness observed in the ﬁt
partition. The categorical variant reference-encodes integer-
valued columns with at most 12 observed levels, using the
most frequent level as reference. Unseen categories map to
that reference. This is a cardinality heuristic rather than a
complete semantic interpretation; higher-cardinality survey
codes remain numeric.
Learning. All six mandatory methods are implemented
with the speciﬁed signatures: MSE GD, single-sample SGD,
normal-equation least squares and ridge, and ordinary and
regularized logistic GD. Their returned loss is evaluated
at the ﬁnal weights and excludes regularization. Ridge
uses X ⊤X + 2N λI, penalizing every supplied coordinate.
Independent checks supplement the ofﬁcial numerical tests.
For prediction we compare linear regression, ridge and
logistic models. Our optional logistic optimizer minimizes

III. M EASURED RESULTS
Table I compares tuning performance; these estimates
are optimistic after selection. The unregularized categorical
model achieves a slightly higher F1 (0.4327), within the
prespeciﬁed tolerance for choosing regularization. Table II
instead ﬁxes the threshold at 0.20 to isolate processing
changes. Categorical encoding provides the largest process-
ing gain; median imputation and missing indicators have
much smaller effects. Increasing L2 to 10−2 lowers tuned
F1 to 0.4099, so stronger regularization is not uniformly
beneﬁcial. Equal optimizer budgets do not imply equal
convergence, which limits attribution of small differences
solely to feature representation.
On the untouched holdout, the ﬁxed selection obtains
F1 0.4225, accuracy 0.8685, balanced accuracy 0.7223,
precision 0.3451, and recall 0.5446. There are 3,156 true
positives, 5,989 false positives, 2,639 false negatives and
53,843 true negatives. ROC-AUC is 0.8585 and average
precision is 0.3861. Training F1 is 0.4392 versus tuning
0.4321; the holdout gap remains a limitation rather than
evidence of an unbiased tuning estimate.
At threshold 0.50, this same holdout model gives F1
0.2444 and accuracy 0.9142. Using the tuning-selected 0.20
threshold improves holdout F1 by 0.1781 while reducing
accuracy by 0.0456; it increases recall from 0.1572 to
0.5446. Figure 1 shows the corresponding tuning tradeoff.
An accuracy-selected threshold of 0.58 instead gives tuning
accuracy 0.9153 and F1 0.1796, underscoring the unresolved
competition objective.

IV. L IMITATIONS AND REPRODUCTION
Survey nonresponse, optional modules, self-report and
shared sampling structure limit interpretation. False nega-
tives remain substantial (45.54% of positives), and a recall-
oriented threshold introduces many false positives. We did
not establish prospective validity, fairness across popula-
tions, full semantic cleaning, or coefﬁcient-wise stability.
Small ablation gains are not established as signiﬁcant or
consistent across folds; only the selected conﬁguration has
the three-fold diagnostic. The 120-step ﬁnal ﬁt also stops
before convergence.
After freezing choices, we reﬁt processing and weights on
all labeled rows, producing 1,183 design columns because
the larger ﬁt discovers more categories. run.py loads
the saved weights and ﬁtted transformations; seed 433 and
threshold 0.20 reproduce the CSV . From the repository
root, run python run.py with -data-dir dataset ,
-output new.csv , and -seed 433 . Two executions,
including a fresh source directory and CSV cache, produce
identical SHA-256 hashes in the same Python 3.12.14 /
NumPy 2.5.3 environment. Cross-backend bitwise iden-
tity is unveriﬁed. Training code, indices, conﬁgurations,
source/data ﬁngerprints and measurements are saved. No
AIcrowd acceptance or score is claimed.
